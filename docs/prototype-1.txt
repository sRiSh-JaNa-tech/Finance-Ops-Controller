AI Finance Controller - Prototype 1

1. Problem framing

Financial transactions are represented differently across banks, payment gateways, accounting systems, invoices, and settlement platforms. The same real-world transaction may have different identifiers, descriptions, dates, formats, fees, currencies, or partial records.

The prototype will determine whether records from different systems refer to the same underlying financial event. It will classify each case as:

- Matched: sufficient evidence confirms that the records represent the same transaction.
- Exception: evidence indicates a conflict, duplicate, missing record, reversal, amount mismatch, or anomaly.
- Uncertain: the evidence is incomplete or contradictory, so the case is escalated for human review.

The central research question is:

Can an AI-based reconciliation agent reliably link heterogeneous financial records, detect reconciliation exceptions, explain its evidence, and abstain when the available evidence is insufficient?

This positions the project as AI-assisted financial verification rather than simple CSV comparison.

2. Research synthesis

The papers suggest a connected pipeline:

- Semantic models and open-banking research provide a common representation for heterogeneous financial data.
- Entity-resolution research provides methods for linking customers, merchants, invoices, accounts, and transaction references.
- Blocking and progressive matching reduce the number of comparisons and prioritize likely matches.
- RPA and workflow-automation research supports automated ingestion, validation, reconciliation, and exception routing.
- Settlement and interchange research explains fees, partial captures, late settlement, reversals, refunds, and net-versus-gross amounts.
- Anomaly and fraud-detection research helps identify suspicious records beyond ordinary non-matches.
- Multi-agent research motivates specialized agents coordinated by a central controller.
- LLM-based entity-matching research supports reasoning over noisy descriptions and unstructured references.
- Audit-control and explainability research requires traceable evidence and human oversight.
- Entity-resolution metrics research supports rigorous evaluation beyond overall accuracy.

The main connection is:

Semantic normalization makes records comparable; entity resolution finds candidate relationships; financial rules validate consistency; anomaly detection identifies suspicious deviations; and an uncertainty-aware controller decides whether to accept, reject, or escalate the result.

3. High-level architecture

3.1 Synthetic source generation

Generate realistic data for:

- Bank transactions
- Payment-gateway events
- Invoices and accounting records
- Settlement reports
- Customer and merchant master records

Inject realistic problems, including different date formats and time zones, spelling variations, missing or corrupted identifiers, duplicates, delayed settlement, partial payments, fees, taxes, reversals, refunds, currency conversion differences, one-to-many relationships, and suspicious transaction patterns.

Every generated record should retain a hidden ground-truth transaction identifier for evaluation.

3.2 Canonical data model

Convert records into a unified transaction schema containing:

- Transaction, invoice, and payment identifiers
- Amount, currency, fee, tax, and net amount
- Transaction date and settlement date
- Customer and merchant references
- Description or narrative
- Source system
- Transaction type
- Reversal or refund status

The original source record and normalized record must both be preserved so every decision can be audited.

3.3 Entity and reference resolution

Resolve common entities such as customers, merchants, accounts, invoices, payments, and settlement batches using a layered strategy:

1. Exact matching on trusted identifiers.
2. Normalized matching on dates, amounts, and references.
3. Blocking to create a manageable candidate set.
4. Fuzzy and semantic matching for names and descriptions.
5. LLM-assisted reasoning only for difficult candidate pairs.

The LLM should not compare every record with every other record. It should receive a small, pre-filtered candidate set together with structured evidence.

3.4 Reconciliation engine

For every candidate relationship, calculate evidence features such as:

- Identifier agreement
- Amount difference
- Date difference
- Customer or merchant agreement
- Invoice-reference similarity
- Currency compatibility
- Fee-adjusted amount compatibility
- Reversal or refund consistency
- Source reliability
- Duplicate likelihood

A conceptual score is:

S = wiI + waA + wdD + weE + wrR - wcC

where I is identifier agreement, A is amount compatibility, D is date compatibility, E is entity/reference similarity, R is rule consistency, and C is the contradiction score. The weights should be tuned or learned using validation data.

The engine must support more than one-to-one matching, including one invoice to multiple payments, one payment split across invoices, gross payment to net settlement, and original payment to reversal.

3.5 Specialized agents

- Ingestion agent: loads and validates source files.
- Normalization agent: maps fields into the canonical schema.
- Entity-resolution agent: resolves customers, merchants, and references.
- Matching agent: proposes transaction links.
- Financial-rules agent: checks amounts, fees, dates, currencies, and reversals.
- Anomaly agent: detects duplicates, unusual patterns, and unexplained differences.
- Evidence agent: constructs the explanation and audit trail.
- Controller agent: combines results and selects Matched, Exception, or Uncertain.

Deterministic rules should control high-risk financial constraints. LLMs should primarily interpret unstructured text and organize evidence, not silently override accounting rules.

3.6 Confidence and abstention

Confidence must be calibrated rather than being an arbitrary LLM-generated number.

- High confidence with no contradictions: Matched.
- Strong contradiction or anomaly: Exception.
- Weak evidence, conflicting agents, or two similarly likely candidates: Uncertain.

Each decision should include evidence. For example:

Matched because the invoice reference and merchant matched, the amount difference was explained by a known processing fee, and settlement occurred within the expected two-day window.

Uncertain because two candidate invoices have similar amounts and descriptions, but neither has a reliable identifier.

4. Prototype workflow

1. Generate or load synthetic records from multiple financial sources.
2. Validate and normalize all source records.
3. Resolve entities and construct candidate transaction pairs.
4. Apply deterministic financial rules.
5. Apply fuzzy, embedding, or LLM matching where necessary.
6. Detect anomalies and contradictions.
7. Combine evidence into a calibrated confidence score.
8. Produce Matched, Exception, or Uncertain.
9. Store the decision, evidence, model outputs, and source records in an audit log.
10. Send uncertain cases to a human-review queue.

5. Evaluation design

Use at least 50-100 records, stratified by case type rather than using only easy examples. Include clean matches, noisy matches, duplicates, missing records, timing differences, fee differences, reversals, partial payments, and anomalies.

Measure:

- Match precision and recall
- False-match rate
- Exception-detection precision and recall
- Uncertain-case precision
- Confidence calibration
- Percentage automatically resolved
- Percentage escalated to human review
- Processing time and throughput
- Explanation completeness
- Performance by error category

Compare the proposed system with:

1. Exact identifier matching.
2. Rule-based matching.
3. Fuzzy matching.
4. A classical machine-learning classifier.
5. A hybrid rules-plus-embeddings system.
6. The proposed agentic system.

The target should not be maximum automation at any cost. A trustworthy system should maximize correct automation while keeping false matches very low.

A useful objective is:

Utility = correct-automation benefit - false-match cost - human-review cost

This incorporates the cost-benefit perspective from progressive entity-matching research.

6. Expected contribution

Prototype 1 will demonstrate a hybrid, evidence-aware financial reconciliation architecture combining semantic normalization, entity resolution, financial consistency rules, anomaly detection, calibrated confidence, and human escalation.

Its contribution is the integration of these components around uncertainty and auditability. The system should demonstrate that it can reconcile heterogeneous records, explain why records were linked, distinguish mismatches from anomalies, recognize when evidence is insufficient, and route ambiguous cases to human reviewers.

The result is a prototype for reliable AI-assisted financial verification, not a claim that an LLM can independently replace financial controls.
